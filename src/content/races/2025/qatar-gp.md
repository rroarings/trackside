# Qatar Grand Prix 2025

## Race Highlights

- Max Verstappen claimed victory from third on the grid, matching the season win tally of both McLaren drivers
- Oscar Piastri secured pole position for both the sprint and the main race, ultimately finishing second in the Grand Prix
- Carlos Sainz Jr. finished third, becoming the first Williams driver since 2015 to achieve multiple podiums in a single season
- Lando Norris finished second in the sprint and fourth in the Grand Prix, maintaining a 12-point lead in the championship
- Pirelli enforced a mandatory 25-lap limit on tire sets, requiring all drivers to make at least two pit stops during the 57-lap race
- Kimi Antonelli secured fifth place for Mercedes after a late-race battle with Norris
- Isack Hadjar and Lance Stroll were the race's only late retirements, with Hadjar suffering a puncture while in a points-scoring position

## Race Summary

Max Verstappen delivered a clinical performance at the 2025 Qatar Grand Prix, winning the penultimate race of the season and keeping the title fight alive heading into the finale. Despite starting behind the McLaren front-row lockout, Verstappen capitalized on a strong start to overtake Lando Norris immediately. A mid-race safety car, triggered by a collision between Pierre Gasly and Nico Hülkenberg, forced a strategic scramble as teams managed the strict 25-lap tire life limit.

The race was defined by high-stakes tire strategy and championship permutations. McLaren's decision not to pit their drivers during the early safety car period backfired, forcing Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris to fight back through the field on fresher rubber in the closing stages. While Piastri recovered to second, Norris was only able to climb back to fourth after passing Kimi Antonelli on the penultimate lap.

The results at the Lusail International Circuit set up a historic three-way title showdown for the final round in Abu Dhabi. Lando Norris leads the standings with 408 points, but his advantage has been cut to just 12 points over Verstappen and 16 points over Piastri. This marks the first time in 15 years that three different drivers enter the final race of the season with a mathematical chance of winning the World Drivers' Championship.
